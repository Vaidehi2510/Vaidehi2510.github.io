01 Patchsentry Trust, evidence & the human in the loop +

A QA workspace that helps developers decide whether to merge. Five AI specialists investigate a pull request; real test results determine its pass or fail status.

My role
Product designer & builder
Context
2026 · AI developer tools
Platform
Web app & GitHub integration

The problem

An AI reviewer can sound confident whether it has evidence or is guessing. Developers need to know what was tested, what was inferred, and what needs their judgment before they merge.

Team leads also need control over the models that can access private code.

Make the evidence readable

  1. Separate test results from AI findings. Model opinions cannot override a failing test.
  2. Show where a finding came from. Link to code, a before-and-after diff, and a suggested regression test. Tie every verdict to the exact revision reviewed.
  3. Reveal detail when it is useful. Start with a clear status and check summary; organize deeper findings into Behavior, Interface, Security, and Boundary case.

Keep people in control

When requirements are ambiguous, the review asks a person to clarify. Teams choose a model and investigation limits for each specialist, including local models with no cloud fallback for confidential code.

The four-step onboarding follows the actual job: connect a product, build a QA team, choose a change, and decide with evidence.

Build the behavior, then check it

I used Codex and Claude Code to build the review flow and try it on real pull requests. That made the labels, evidence, and requests for human input part of a working product.

The project includes 278 automated tests. Its axe-core checks found zero accessibility violations on the desktop and mobile pages tested.

02 AURORA & Watchpost One urgent decision. Two different contexts. +

An early-warning experience for critical infrastructure, first as a web dashboard with spoken alerts, then rethought as a private, voice-first app for iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

My role
Product designer & builder
Context
DC Critical Ops Hackathon 2026 → independent iteration
Platform
Web · iOS · iPadOS · macOS

The problem

Network, physical security, and threat intelligence teams watch separate screens. The early signals of an incident can be scattered across all three.

An operator needs to understand the situation and decide whether to escalate, including when they are on the floor and away from a desk.

Version 1: design for the decision

  1. Put the urgent information first. Show the headline, priority, and confidence before evidence, historical context, and an escalation recommendation.
  2. Let the interface speak. Read critical alerts aloud and support spoken follow-up questions. The target was under 60 seconds from raw signal to spoken assessment.
  3. Make system state visible. Show when voice and AI are ready, and keep the dashboard usable when AI is unavailable.

What the demo changed

In the demo scenario, giving the AI analyst an annotated camera frame changed its confidence from 60 to 80 and its recommendation from monitoring to immediate escalation.

I demoed AURORA to judges and attendees and carried their feedback into the next iteration. A larger question emerged: how could the experience work away from a screen, without sending sensitive data to outside models?

Version 2: meet the operator where they are

Watchpost brings triage into Siri, Shortcuts, Spotlight, and the Mac menu bar. Seven phrases across three shortcuts work without setup.

AI runs on the device. A rules engine provides briefs when Apple Intelligence is unavailable, and fixed rules always determine the priority score. VoiceOver labels, empty states, and swipe actions support the experience across devices.

03 WARBREAK Making complex tradeoffs tangible +

An AI planning tool that finds the assumptions hidden in a plan, then turns them into a simulation. People make choices, watch the consequences, and explore why their plan failed.

My role
Product designer & builder
Context
SCSP AI+ Expo 2026 · Wargaming Track
Platform
Web · interactive maps & data visualization

The problem

Plans can fail on assumptions nobody wrote down. A report listing those assumptions is easy to skim and forget.

I wanted people to experience which assumption breaks first, through decisions they make themselves.

Four screens, one learning loop

  1. Plan. Write in plain English or choose a scenario. AI extracts assumptions and scores their fragility from 0 to 100.
  2. Prepare. Choose from twelve assets within a fixed budget.
  3. Play. Take turns on a live map against an adversary targeting the weakest assumption.
  4. Reflect. Review what failed, why it failed, and how another timeline could unfold.

Make each decision matter

The number of turns equals the number of assets selected, so resources and time are linked. Six live metrics show consequences for public opinion, domestic and allied support, and force strength.

Animated strikes, sonar rings, cyber pulses, and movement make the result of a turn visible on the map. A move that succeeds in one dimension can still weaken the overall plan.

Explain the loss

The seven-tab Failure Autopsy connects assumptions to decisions and consequences. It includes the decision log, adversary reasoning, a metrics verdict, failure chains, alternative timelines, and recommendations.

The interaction carries the explanation: make a choice, see its effects, then trace those effects back to the assumption that made the plan fragile.
