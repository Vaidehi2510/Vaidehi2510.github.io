Case study 01 · Designing an AI-powered experience

Patchsentry: AI review a team can trust

A QA workspace where five AI specialists review every pull request, and real test results, not the model, decide whether it passes.

Role
Product designer and builder
Year
2026
Platform
Web app and GitHub integration
Built with
Codex, Claude Code, Next.js

The problem

AI code reviewers sound equally confident when they are right and when they are guessing. A developer deciding whether to merge cannot tell what was actually tested from what a model assumed. Teams with private code also cannot send it to just any cloud model.

Who it is for

Developers and reviewers making a merge decision on a pull request, and the team leads who set up which checks and models run.

Key decisions

  • Separate facts from opinions. Test results establish pass or fail. Findings from the AI team are labeled separately and can never override real test evidence.
  • Every finding shows its source. Each one links to the code or evidence behind it, with a before-and-after diff and a suggested regression test, and every verdict is tied to the exact revision inspected.
  • Ask instead of guessing. When a requirement is ambiguous, the review stops and asks a human to clarify.
  • Status first, detail on demand. The review opens on a plain status ("Attention needed" or "Review complete") and a summary of checks. Tabs split detail into Behavior, Interface, Security and Boundary case.
  • Onboarding that mirrors the job. Four steps: connect your product, build your QA team, choose a change, decide with evidence.
  • Control over the model. Teams build their QA team from five specialists (test planner, code reviewer, security reviewer, UI/UX reviewer, bug triage), set how far each may investigate, and pick a model for each role: hosted, or local with no cloud fallback for confidential code.
  • Honest copy. The product says plainly that no model can guarantee it finds every bug, and that coverage depends on your tests and journeys.

How AI shaped the work

AI is the material and the tool. I designed how model output is presented, labeled and challenged, and I built the product with Codex and Claude Code, which let me test the real review flow on real pull requests instead of static mockups.

Accessibility and quality

Patchsentry's own browser checks run axe-core accessibility audits on desktop and mobile viewports. The web app passed with zero violations on the pages tested.

Case study 02 · Multimodal AI, voice, iteration

AURORA and Watchpost: one alert, designed twice

An early-warning system for critical infrastructure, first as a hackathon web dashboard with spoken alerts, then redesigned as a private, voice-first app for iPhone, iPad and Mac.

Role
Product designer and builder
Context
DC Critical Ops Hackathon 2026, then independent
Platform
Web dashboard; iOS, iPadOS, macOS
Built with
React, FastAPI, LLMs; SwiftUI, App Intents

The problem

Network, physical security and threat intelligence teams each watch their own screen. The signals that come before an attack on a substation are often spread across all three, and nobody connects them in time.

Who it is for

Operators watching critical sites, who may be on the floor rather than at a desk, and who need to decide quickly whether to escalate to incident response.

Version 1: AURORA

  • Lead with the decision. Alert cards open with headline, priority and confidence. Evidence, the closest historical match, the AI analyst's verdict and an escalation recommendation follow in that order.
  • Speak when it matters. Critical alerts are read aloud. Operators can ask follow-up questions by voice and hear the answer, so their eyes and hands stay free. The target was under 60 seconds from raw signal to a spoken assessment.
  • Show the AI what a person would see. The analyst reviews the camera frame with the anomaly boxed. In the demo, seeing the frame moved its verdict from "possible threat, 60, do not escalate" to "probable threat, 80, escalate to IR immediately."
  • Make system state visible. Voice and AI status indicators show READY before an operator relies on them, and the dashboard keeps working in a reduced mode when the AI is unavailable.

What I learned from the demo

I demoed AURORA to hackathon judges and attendees and folded their feedback into the next iteration. The bigger lesson was about context: a cloud dashboard asks an operator to be at a screen and to send sensitive data to outside models.

Version 2: Watchpost

  • Meet people where they already are. Three Siri shortcuts work with zero setup, using seven phrases such as "Triage VPN outage in Watchpost." Spotlight results open the exact incident, and a menu bar glance covers the Mac.
  • Private by default. All AI runs on the device, with zero network calls.
  • Never a dead end. On devices without Apple Intelligence, a rules engine writes the brief, so every user gets an answer.
  • Explainable ranking. The 0–100 priority score always comes from fixed rules, even when a model writes the summary, so the same incident always ranks the same way.
  • Built for everyone. VoiceOver labels, empty states and swipe actions across iPhone, iPad and Mac.

Case study 03 · Interaction design for complex analysis

WARBREAK: the game is the interface

An AI planning tool that finds the hidden assumptions in a plan, then lets you play it out against an adversary that attacks the weakest one.

Role
Product designer and builder
Context
SCSP AI+ Expo 2026, Wargaming Track
Platform
Web
Built with
Next.js, Leaflet, FastAPI, Gemini

The problem

Plans fail on assumptions nobody wrote down. A report listing those assumptions is easy to skim and forget. I wanted people to feel which assumption breaks first.

The flow

Write or pick a plan in plain English. AI pulls out its hidden assumptions and scores each one 0–100 for fragility. Choose assets under a budget, play turns on a live map, then read an autopsy of what failed and why.

Key decisions

  • Budgets create real choices. Twelve assets compete for a fixed budget, and the number of turns equals the number of assets you pick, so every choice costs something.
  • Show consequences beyond the obvious score. Six live metrics track public opinion, domestic and allied support and force strength, so a move that wins on the map can still lose overall.
  • Make the map talk. Strikes, sonar rings, cyber pulses and ground movement animate on the map, so each move has visible feedback.
  • Explain the loss. A seven-tab Failure Autopsy covers the assumption chain, the decision log, the adversary's reasoning on each turn, the failure chain, a what-if comparison of your timeline with a better one, and recommendations.

How I work

AI is part of every step

  1. Start with the person

    Talk to the people with the problem and write down what they need. At OpenTeams I turned a user's migration needs into a gap analysis of what was supported, partial and missing.

  2. Sketch the flow

    Map the journey and the key screens in Figma, and decide what each screen must answer first.

  3. Prototype for real

    Build a working version with Claude Code, Codex or Cursor, so people react to real behavior and real data instead of static frames.

  4. Test and refine

    Put it in front of people, run accessibility audits on desktop and mobile, and change what the evidence says to change.
